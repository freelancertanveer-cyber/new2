# Equipment Finance Brokers — Cold Email Copy Pack

**Industry pick:** `equipment_finance_brokers_sme`

**Why this one next (over the remaining 4):**

- Equipment lease/loan maturity is the **single biggest trigger for "let's explore financing alternatives"** — a $200k–$2M equipment package with 18–24 months left is actively on an operations manager or CFO's radar. Equipment finance brokers own the origination, and timing is everything.
- TAM is solid and fragmented: thousands of independent equipment finance shops, captive lender brokers, leasing consultants, and finance brokers across the US. Every metro has 50–200+ on Google Maps + LinkedIn actively hunting new equipment deals.
- Deal size and commissions are substantial ($5k–$50k per placement), so they can absolutely afford $1.5k–$3.5k/month for high-signal equipment borrower pipeline.
- SME operations managers and CFOs holding equipment financing are *underrepresented* in Apollo/ZoomInfo datasets vs. corporate procurement — but they are visible on Google Maps (businesses with visible equipment), business registries, lease registries, and state filings. Fresh data = origination advantage.
- The broker side is starved for *proprietary* deal flow. Lenders and brokers all fight over the same LinkedIn equipment-buyer profiles — we hand them companies that haven't listed their equipment-renewal intent anywhere yet. Zero pattern fatigue.
- The equipment-maturity signal works *better* here than in many niches because the decision isn't "should we explore this lender" — it's "we *have* to refinance or replace equipment in 18 months." That is a deal-ready conversation.

## The offer stack we are pitching (in order)

1. **Front-end (easy yes):** 25–50 company-profile records with upcoming equipment financing maturity + estimated revenue and equipment type — free, no call, no deposit.
2. **Pilot:** 100–300 verified SME companies with equipment financing due in 12–24 months, full business + decision-maker contact data, operations/finance contact info — flat fee.
3. **Full campaign (unlocked after trust):** Google-Maps + lease-registry-sourced SME company profiles + fully-managed outbound deal-flow pipeline doing ~400k sends/month from pre-warmed inboxes, 2–3 qualified equipment buyer convos per week expected, priced at low infra cost.

The first two emails only sell #1. #3 shows up in email 3 and in reply threads.

## Framework applied (from the Lead Gen Jay video)

Every sequence below is engineered against the **Triple-Tap**:

1. **Open** — Preview text (subject + first line) sounds like a peer/vendor/fellow deal-maker, sparks curiosity, does not telegraph "cold email," relates to the body.
2. **Read** — 2–4 sentences max. Specific pain → unique mechanism → tiny proof.
3. **Reply** — One-handed "yes / send it / sure." No calendar. No 30-min call ask.

Rules I held to:
- No "quick question," no "hope you're well," no "just checking in."
- No generic "I help X do Y" openers.
- No CTA that requires them to think, schedule, or write more than one word.
- Front-end offer carries the unique mechanism (SME company profiles + equipment-maturity trigger) — not the back-end service.
- Psyche match: Equipment finance broker language ("your equipment buyer pipeline," "lease renewal book," "financing coming due," "equipment replacement," "deal flow," "operations managers").

## Implementation notes

- **Equipment-maturity data sources:** Public lease registries, UCC filings (equipment collateral), equipment finance company public records, state and county business filings, commercial vehicle registries.
- **Overlap with existing campaigns:** If running this alongside business loans or CRE, segment by industry and company size first — manufacturers and construction firms may overlap, which is fine if timing is staggered >2 weeks.
- **Quality bar:** Only include companies with verifiable equipment lease or financing maturity dates within 12–24 months. Cross-reference against public lease end dates to ensure accuracy. No "estimated" maturity guesses.
- **Contact validation:** Always validate operations manager/finance contact email via company website + LinkedIn + state filings before sending. Skip profiles where you can only find a generic inbox address or equipment supplier contact.
